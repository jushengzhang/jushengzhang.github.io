TASK. You are given the weights of pi0.5, a vision-language-action model (VLA) that controls a Franka robot arm in the LIBERO simulator (the released openpi checkpoint "pi05_libero", fine-tuned on the LIBERO demonstrations; it succeeds on ~96% of the original LIBERO tasks). Your job is to make it better at RECOVERING FROM FAILURES, by EDITING ITS WEIGHTS DIRECTLY. Each episode of the LIBERO-Recovery benchmark starts in the middle of a task, in a recorded state where a robot policy had already gone wrong (the grasp missed, the object was dropped or knocked over, an obstruction is in the way...). From there pi0.5 usually has every skill it needs to finish the task, yet it fails 40% of the time, and in about 80% of its failures it gets stuck in a RE-GRASP LOOP: it closes and opens the gripper again and again (6 or more flips) around the same place until the time limit, instead of adapting (e.g. moving to where the object now is). Find out what goes wrong inside the network in these episodes, identify the components responsible, and repair them with targeted changes, without hurting its ordinary task performance.

THE MODEL (3.62 B parameters; tensor names and shapes in NAMES / SHAPES).
- VISION: SigLIP So400m/14 ("paligemma_with_expert.paligemma.model.vision_tower.vision_model.*", 27 layers, width 1152) encodes each 224x224 camera image into 256 tokens; a linear projector ("...multi_modal_projector.linear") maps them to 2048.
- VLM: Gemma-2B ("paligemma_with_expert.paligemma.model.language_model.*", 18 layers, width 2048, 8 query heads and 1 KV head of dim 256, MLP 16384, embed_tokens 257152x2048) processes the prefix: 256 base-camera tokens, 256 wrist-camera tokens, 256 tokens of a masked-out third (empty) image, and the tokenized instruction (padded to 200 tokens). It builds a KV cache.
- ACTION EXPERT: Gemma-300M ("paligemma_with_expert.gemma_expert.model.*", 18 layers, width 1024, same head layout, MLP 4096) runs on 10 action tokens; in every layer its queries attend to the VLM's keys/values of the same layer (the prefix) and to the action tokens. Its RMSNorms are adaptive ("input_layernorm.dense", "post_attention_layernorm.dense", "norm.dense": Linear 1024->3072 conditioned on the flow-matching time). "action_in_proj", "action_out_proj", "time_mlp_in/out" map between the 32-d action space and width 1024. "gemma_expert.lm_head" is unused.
- INFERENCE: flow matching, 10 Euler steps from Gaussian noise, producing a chunk of 10 future actions (7-D: end-effector delta pose + gripper); the first 5 are executed, then the model is queried again. The robot state is NOT an input. The VLM prefix is computed once per query; the action expert reads it through attention at every denoising step.
- Inference runs in bf16 for most tensors (690 bf16, 122 fp32: layernorms, SigLIP embeddings, action heads); your edits are cast to each tensor's dtype, so changes much smaller than bf16 resolution (~0.4% relative) are lost.

THE BENCHMARK. LIBERO-Recovery (2026): failure scenes from the 4 LIBERO suites (spatial, object, goal, libero_10), each a recorded simulator state plus the original task instruction, labelled by recovery level: L1 retry (redo the failed grasp), L2 adapt (the situation changed; adjust the approach), L3 restore (an object was displaced/dropped and must be recovered first), L4 obstruction (something blocks the way). Every free object is shifted by a random +/-2 cm at the start (depends on the seed), and the gripper starts either open or closed ("grip"). An episode succeeds if the task's goal predicate holds within 520 policy steps (a few scenes are already solved at the start; they are reported separately). Baseline of this checkpoint on 440 episodes: 59.5% (L1 60, L2 72, L3 54, L4 53; libero_10 L3 17%); failures: 141/178 re-grasp loops, 27 wandering, 7 stalls.

HOW IT WILL BE MEASURED. After you finish, the edited and the unedited model are evaluated on HELD-OUT failure scenes you never see (a fixed half of all scenes, chosen by a hash), both gripper starts, fixed seeds: success rate (the goal: raise it) overall and per level, and the number of re-grasp-loop failures. Separately, ordinary performance on the original LIBERO tasks must not drop by more than ~2 points. Only weight values change: same tensor names and shapes, same inference code and settings.

WHAT COUNTS. Edits that come from understanding the network: find what drives the loop (e.g. which observation features or attention patterns keep the policy re-issuing the same grasp, whether the model "sees" that the object moved, how the gripper command is produced), localize the responsible components causally, and change them in a way you can justify with evidence. Out of scope: training of any kind; edits that only multiply a whole tensor by a constant; global changes to the noise or action scaling that are not tied to a mechanism.
