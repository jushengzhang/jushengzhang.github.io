Jusheng Zhang

I am a Visiting Researcher at Stanford University, working with Joseph C. Wu and Xuekun Wu on agentic AI for life sciences, and a Student Researcher at Google DeepMind, working with Ming-Hsuan Yang and Liangzhe Yuan on efficient video and vision–language models.

My research connects multi-agent collaboration, reasoning under uncertainty, efficient visual intelligence, and embodied decision-making.

Previously, I worked with teams at Nanyang Technological University, Meta AI, Snap Research, and the HCP Lab of Sun Yat-sen University. Earlier, I worked on risk-control algorithms at Ant Group. More about me ↗

My goal is to help build the next generation of AI agents for scientific discovery.

  • Markets of Minds: A 51-Billion-State Economy of Intelligence

    Allocating expert-model assistance across agents by expected benefit and regression risk.

    Jusheng Zhang, Xuekun Wu, Wenhao Wang, Mark Wang, Ming-Hsuan Yang

    arXiv 2026 (Preprint)

    [Paper] [Code]

    Treats expert-model assistance across a population of agents as a constrained allocation problem, scoring each agent-service pair by expected gain minus regression risk before any expert output is observed.

  • Failure-Driven Workflow Refinement

    Turning recurring failure modes into targeted, verified edits to agent workflows.

    Jusheng Zhang, Kaitong Cai, Qinglin Zeng, Ningyuan Liu, Stephen Fan, Jing Yang, Ziliang Chen, Yongsen Zheng, Kwok-Yan Lam, Liang Lin, Keze Wang

    Qinglin Zeng, Ningyuan Liu, and Stephen Fan also contributed to this work; I apologize for inadvertently omitting them from the initial homepage credits, which have now been corrected.

    ICML 2026

    [Paper] [arXiv] [Code]

    ICML 2026 Spotlight

    Reframes LLM workflow optimization as reducing a workflow's expected failure mass: CE-Graph clusters counterexamples into failure modes and applies verified graph edits that shrink them.

  • KABB: Knowledge-Aware Bayesian Bandits for Dynamic Expert Coordination

    Matching tasks to expert agents with knowledge-aware Bayesian bandit selection.

    Jusheng Zhang, Zimeng Huang, Yijia Fan, Ningyuan Liu, Mingyan Li, Zhuojie Yang, Jiawei Yao, Jian Wang, Keze Wang

    ICML 2025

    [Paper] [arXiv] [Code]

    KABB selects which expert agents to use for a task via Thompson Sampling guided by a semantic knowledge-distance model, with dual adaptation that keeps expert representations current.

  • GAM-Agent: Game-Theoretic and Uncertainty-Aware Collaboration for Complex Visual Reasoning

    Combining specialized agents and a critic through game-theoretic, uncertainty-aware collaboration.

    Jusheng Zhang, Yijia Fan, Wenjun Lin, Ruiqi Chen, Haoyi Jiang, Wenhao Chai, Jian Wang, Keze Wang

    NeurIPS 2025

    [Paper] [arXiv] [Code]

    Base agents handle visual sub-tasks while a critic agent checks consistency; modeled as a non-zero-sum game, they exchange claims and uncertainties and debate when they disagree.

  • Self-Rewarded Multimodal Coherent Reasoning Across Diverse Visual Domains

    Using self-derived coherence and grounding signals to reward multimodal reasoning.

    Jusheng Zhang, Ningyuan Liu, Kaitong Cai, Sidi Liu, Ziliang Chen, Wenhao Wang, Keze Wang

    NeurIPS 2026

    A label-free reinforcement learning framework that rewards multimodal LLM reasoning chains using five self-derived coherence and grounding signals, improving accuracy and step consistency without a critic.

  • Hybrid Token Compression for Vision-Language Models

    Compressing visual tokens with continuous features and discrete semantic anchors.

    Jusheng Zhang, Xiaoyang Guo, Kaitong Cai, Qinhan Lv, Yijia Fan, Wenhao Chai, Jian Wang, Keze Wang

    CVPR 2026

    [Paper] [arXiv] [Code]

    CVPR 2026 Highlight

    Compresses a VLM's hundreds of visual tokens into a single token by fusing continuous patch features with a few discrete semantic anchor tokens, retaining most benchmark performance.

  • CF-VLM: CounterFactual Vision-Language Fine-tuning

    Learning from counterfactual image–text pairs to distinguish minimal visual changes.

    Jusheng Zhang, Kaitong Cai, Yijia Fan, Jian Wang, Keze Wang

    NeurIPS 2025

    [Paper] [arXiv] [Code]

    Fine-tunes vision-language models with counterfactual image-text pairs so they stay aligned, distinguish factual from counterfactual scenes, and respond to minimal causal edits, improving compositional reasoning.

  • Video Encoders Built on Image Representations

    Building a compact video encoder on image representations with selective temporal context.

    Jusheng Zhang, Wenhao Wang, Longqi Cai, Liangzhe Yuan, Yuxiao Wang, Ming-Hsuan Yang

    arXiv 2026 (Preprint)

    [Paper] [Code]

    Builds a compact video encoder on a frozen image encoder by selecting a fixed token budget across frames and adding temporal context only to selected tokens.

  • Vocabulary Scaling Law: Tuning Open-vocabulary Predictors for Their Openness

    Studying vocabulary scaling and selecting class subsets to improve open-vocabulary prediction.

    Z. Chen, Y. Li, L. Fang, Jusheng Zhang*, Y. Zheng, Q. Guan, X. Chen

    CVPR 2026

    [Paper] [Code]

    CVPR 2026 Highlight

    Shows that an open-vocabulary classifier's robustness to new and distractor classes is bounded by its full-vocabulary performance, and fine-tunes on a submodularly chosen class subset.
